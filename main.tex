\chapter*{Foreword}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Foreword}
This thesis studies how many edges or arcs a network can contain when no pair of vertices is allowed to have too many independent routes between them.
The starting point is Erd\H{o}s Problem~915, an extremal question about edge-disjoint paths in simple graphs. 
The work begins with the necessary background to formulate the problem and all its variants. 
It then discusses their answers or highlights the difficulty behind unsolved cases. 
Afterwards, computational research on small graphs checks finite instances of these values and supplies dense examples in open cases.
This is accomplished by measuring connectivity with a maximum-flow routine and by searching for extremal graphs with metaheuristics.
Completed exhaustive computations establish an extremal value in a finite search space. Heuristic searches and unfinished enumerations provide only lower bounds, witnessed by the feasible graphs they find.
At the end, the thesis synthesises the results and presents the open problems that remain.
This work is theoretical in nature, but its computational methods, constructions and open questions address the relationship between connectivity and size in networks.

I could not ask for a more patient and enthusiastic supervisor.

\chapter*{Short Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Short Summary}
Erd\H{o}s Problem~915 asks how many edges a simple graph on $n$ vertices can
have if no pair is joined by $m$ edge-disjoint paths
\cite{BollobasErdos62,ErdosProblems}. Writing the maximum as $\ell_m(n)$,
Mader's bound is
\[
 \ell_m(n)\le\floorfrac{m(n-1)}2.
\]
An independent Gomory--Hu proof and matching construction establish equality for $n\ge m\ge2$. The thesis studies sixteen variants in direction, multiplicity, hyperedge size and separation. Vertex separation is measured in the incidence
graph, directed when the model is directed, so separated routes share
neither an intermediate vertex nor an edge copy.

The checked results include the undirected multigraph edge value
$(m-1)(n-1)$, the hypergraph edge bound $\floor{(m-1)(n-1)/(r-1)}$ together
with its simple attainment range, the hypergraph vertex value
$\floor{(n-1)/(r-1)}$ at $m=2$, and the directed value
$\max(2(n-1),\floor{n^2/4})$ at $m=2$ under both separations. The classical simple vertex results agree
with the edge values through $m=4$ and first diverge at $m=5,n=14$
\cite{Bartfai60,Bollobas66,Leonard72b,SorensenThomassen74}.

The checked directed multigraph arc formula is
\[
 L_m^{\mathrm{dir}}(n)=(m-1)\max\!\left(2(n-1),\floorfrac{n^2}4\right).
\]
Its proof repeatedly removes a sparse subgraph preserving reachability.
The checked incidence-rank argument gives the hypergraph vertex upper bound
$\floor{2(n-1)/(r-1)}$ at $m=3$, with equality in the stated attainment
ranges. Checked directed hypergraph arguments establish the leading term
$(m-1)n^2/(4(r-1))$ for fixed $r\ge3,m\ge2$, under both separations and
in the forward, backward and general orientation models.
For simple digraphs, checked elementary arguments give explicit bounds at
every order and the asymptotic form $n^2/4+\Theta_m(n)$ for fixed $m\ge3$.
That asymptotic form also follows from the cited theorem of Huang and Lyu
and the feasible augmented bipartite construction. The exact simple directed
value remains open for $m\ge3$.

Eleven further statements retain the status \emph{conjecture with proposed
proof}, as explained in the Contribution Statement. They concern block
reductions, multigraph incidence bounds and the classification of directed
multigraph extremisers. Their included arguments have not been verified
line by line by the author and are not used as established results.

The companion program uses maximum flow for connectivity, exhaustive
search for finite optima, and tabu search or random greedy growth for
larger instances. Heuristic results are lower bounds. An equal-time
experiment compares unaided search with supplied constructions. The appendix
contains the proofs, proposed proofs and computational audit.
